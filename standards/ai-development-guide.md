## AI Feature Development Guidelines

### ✅ Do's

- Target workflows where users spend most of their time **moving information, not thinking** (copying, uploading, waiting, interpreting)
- Use AI to **collapse steps** in existing workflows, not introduce new behaviors users need to learn
- Turn raw inputs into **clear recommendations** — summarize messy data into one sentence
- **Flag what matters** instead of showing everything
- **Pre-fill decisions** users already make the same way most of the time
- Explain **why** something happened, not just what happened
- Launch with **one core feature done exceptionally well**, then add AI to the most painful part
- Price based on **value delivered** (outcomes), not time saved or market averages
- Prioritize AI in: document review, data cleanup, report generation, status updates, compliance checks, intake and triage workflows

### ❌ Don'ts

- Don't use AI to **replace user judgment** — remove friction, not control
- Don't keep **"upload and wait"** as the core experience — that's the gap AI should fill
- Don't target **new behaviors** users need to be taught; go where habits already exist
- Don't justify premium pricing if **manual steps still happen behind the scenes**
- Don't rebuild everything at once — **fix the one thing users hate most** first
- Don't automate decisions users actually **want to make themselves**
- Don't collapse the step users take **pride in**. Competence is the psychological need most tied to long-term motivation, so aim AI at the step they resent, not the one that makes them feel good at their job

### 🎯 Best AI Fit Signals (when planning a feature)

- Users are copying/pasting the same type of content repeatedly
- The app shows data but forces users to decide what it means
- There are long instructions explaining how to interpret results
- The core value is "processing" or "analysis" but relies on manual steps
- Users trust the output but hate the effort required to get there
- The workflow contains one step that earns pride and one that earns resentment, and the two are cleanly separable
