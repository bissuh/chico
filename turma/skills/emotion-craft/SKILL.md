# emotion-craft

Content spreads on emotion, not information. The feed measures how hard and how fast people react, which is the same as measuring emotional intensity. A post that makes someone feel mildly interested gets a like. A post that makes them feel something strong gets a comment, a save, a share. The job is not to inform. It is to make the reader feel something powerful enough that they cannot scroll past.

This skill is the emotional charge. `turma:story-craft` is the structure (the 5-line arc). emotion-craft is the lever you run through it: which feeling, and how to earn it honestly.

**Before you start:** read the project's `brand.md`. You need the audience and their splinter, because the strongest triggers articulate what the reader already feels but has not said.

## The information vs provocation split

Two founders, same expertise. One posts "5 tips for X" and gets 12 likes from the same people every week. The other posts something that makes the reader think "holy shit, that's me," and books calls. Same information. One delivered it. One made people feel it. Every flat piece is on the wrong side of this line.

## The 6 triggers (the palette)

Every piece that spreads pulls at least one hard. The strongest pull two or three. Pick deliberately, do not scatter.

1. **Identity validation.** Make the reader feel seen. Articulate the uncomfortable truth they know but never say out loud. Not the popular opinion dressed as unpopular ("cold email still works"), the real thing under it: "you're not struggling to find clients because of your offer, you're scared to charge what you're worth." Done right, the reader feels called out or vindicated, and either one demands a response.
2. **Status signaling.** People share what makes *them* look good. Content is a social object for the sharer's self-presentation. Ask not "is this valuable" but "what does sharing this say about the person who shares it." Insider knowledge (looks connected), a contrarian take before it is mainstream (looks ahead of the curve), a sharp breakdown (looks competent and generous). Give people a way to say something about themselves they could not say directly.
3. **Tribal belonging.** Draw the line between who this is for and who it is not. "There are two types of founders..." creates an in-group the reader wants to join and an out-group they do not want to be in. Use this one sparingly and honestly (see the guardrails), because it is the trigger most easily abused into cheap us-vs-them.
4. **Productive discomfort.** Cognitive dissonance with a path forward. Expose the gap between the reader's self-image (smart, competent) and their actual result, then imply the gap is closeable. Not criticism, not negativity. Discomfort that motivates, because you also show the way out.
5. **The curiosity gap.** The tension of knowing information exists that you do not have. Intensity beats novelty: "the post that booked me 47 calls in 72 hours" beats "how to get more engagement." Specific numbers, implied insider knowledge, a gap the reader needs closed. This is the foundation of almost every hook.
6. **Aspiration.** Future-self identification. Show a result the reader wants and make them believe it is achievable *for them*. Achievable beats impressive: "0 to $42k in 90 days using only LinkedIn" lands where "$10M in year one" triggers only skepticism. Aspiration without believability is just fantasy.

## The method

1. **Pick a primary trigger.** What is the one feeling you want the reader to have? Firing all six at once makes confusing content.
2. **Pick a secondary.** One more, to deepen it. (Curiosity to hook, aspiration to develop. Or discomfort to hook, validation to develop.)
3. **Hook on the primary.** The first line creates the emotion immediately. Curiosity opens the gap. Validation opens with the recognition. Discomfort opens with the uncomfortable truth.
4. **Develop on the secondary.** Once hooked, deepen with the second feeling.
5. **Close with action.** A question that invites a comment, a handraiser for a magnet (`turma:micromagnet-craft`), or a reframe the reader can use.
6. **Run the guardrails** (below) before it ships.

## Belief disruption (for reaching people who don't know they have a problem)

Most content targets the problem-aware. The most powerful content targets the problem-*unaware* and shifts their belief until they realize they have a problem. Not anger, not manufactured controversy. Disrupting a belief creates internal dissonance the reader has to resolve. Structure:

1. State the common belief plainly. "Most founders think the way to grow is to post valuable content consistently."
2. Create doubt. "But I know founders who did that for a year with under 1,000 followers, and founders who post once a week and book 20 calls a month."
3. Introduce the new frame. "The difference isn't the value. It's the psychology."
4. Show the implication. "Which means most founders work 5x harder for worse results, optimizing for the wrong thing."
5. Offer the path forward (optional). The reframe, or the handraiser.

By the end, a reader who was not problem-aware is.

## The guardrails (this is what separates craft from manipulation)

Every one of these triggers can be used to manipulate. The moment a reader smells manipulation, trust is gone and it backfires. Run every emotionally charged piece through these before it ships. Where a trigger and a guardrail conflict, the guardrail wins.

1. **Is it true?** Not "could be true for someone." True, from real experience. The instant you exaggerate or invent, all trust evaporates.
2. **Would you say it in person?** To a smart peer at dinner. If it would feel try-hard or slimy out loud, it reads that way on the page.
3. **Is the emotion earned?** Proportional to the point. "This will DESTROY your reach" over a posting-time tip is ridiculous. Strong language is earned only by a strong point.
4. **Does it serve the reader or you?** Guru content is self-serving disguised as helpful. If you are triggering emotion to extract engagement rather than to help the reader understand something real, it shows.
5. **Specific over generic.** "Most people do this wrong" is guru energy. "Founders charging under $5k who've posted six months without booking a call" is real. Specificity proves you understand the problem instead of farming a reaction.
6. **Extraordinary claims need proof.** "This books 15 calls a week" triggers skepticism. The same claim with a real screenshot triggers aspiration. Proof converts hype into believable possibility.

**The turma line on top of these:** the house style already bans the cheapest versions of these tricks, and they stay banned. No manufactured controversy, no fake scarcity or fake urgency, no pseudo-contrarian "nobody [verb]" hooks. Use the emotional *truth* (real recognition, earned discomfort, honest aspiration). Skip the manipulation. `turma:anti-ai-linguo` enforces this as the final pass.

## Modes

- **CHOOSE:** given a topic, pick the primary + secondary trigger and draft the hook around them. Hand to `turma:story-craft` or `turma:social-posts` to build.
- **CHARGE:** given a flat draft, name which trigger it is missing and rewrite the hook and frame to fire it, then run the guardrails.
- **DISRUPT:** given a problem-unaware audience, build a belief-disruption piece with the 5-step structure.

## Hard rules

- Reads `brand.md`. The trigger only works if it names what *this* audience actually feels.
- The guardrails are not optional: truth, earned emotion, specificity, proof. Where a trigger conflicts with a guardrail, the guardrail wins.
- No manufactured controversy, fake scarcity, or pseudo-contrarian hooks, ever. Emotional truth only.
- No dashes, no AI tells. Run `turma:anti-ai-linguo` last.
