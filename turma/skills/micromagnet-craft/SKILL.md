# micromagnet-craft

Lead magnets are dead. PDFs that go unopened, free strategy sessions that are obviously sales calls, 5-day challenges that never get finished. A micromagnet is the replacement: a complete solution to a tiny splinter problem, delivered in a vehicle the reader can use in 2 minutes, traded for one easy action.

Every micromagnet earns one thing: the project's primary conversion, whatever `brand.md` names it. That might be an email opt-in, an app signup, a first purchase, a booking, or an install. The scoreboard runs through this skill.

Source material: Tony Allen's MicroMagnet method (the Playbook and the teardown), reduced to operating instructions below. The frameworks are his. The coupling to your project is yours.

**Before you start:** read the project's `brand.md`. The audience splinter, the voice, and the primary conversion goal all come from there. micromagnet-craft supplies the method. `brand.md` supplies the specifics.

## When to invoke

- Drafting any public-facing project content. Check if there's a micromagnet hiding inside it before shipping. Most narrative content has one. Most lists definitely do.
- Designing a micromagnet from scratch around a known reader splinter.
- Reviewing an existing magnet (or magnet-shaped artifact) to see if it earns its keep.
- Writing the social handraiser post that promotes a finished micromagnet.
- Anytime the question is "how do we get this content to convert, not just collect claps."

## When NOT to invoke

- Any surface `brand.md` reserves for the owner's own voice. That surface is the anchor and doesn't need a CTA bolt-on. Don't add one unsolicited.
- Pure operational artifacts: weekly reviews, internal docs, session notes, memory files.
- Pieces explicitly framed as "no CTA" reader gifts. Sometimes generosity is the point.
- When a story pass hasn't happened yet on narrative content. Story first, micromagnet after. The magnet rides on the loop the story opens. Run `turma:story-craft` first.

## Three modes

Pick one. State it at the top of your output.

### Mode 1: CREATE

Build a micromagnet from scratch around a known reader problem. Inputs:
- A reader splinter (specific, painful, 2-minute-solvable)
- A target outcome (the primary conversion from `brand.md`; a different action like "reply with WORD" only if the owner requests it)
- Context about who the reader is (the audience from `brand.md`)

Output: title + delivery vehicle + the actual artifact (or a tight build spec if it's a GPT/skill) + the social handraiser post that promotes it.

### Mode 2: EXTRACT

Mine a piece of content already in flight (newsletter draft, thread, video script) for a micromagnet hiding inside it. Inputs:
- The draft
- The current CTA, if any

Output: the splinter the content already touches, the magnet that pops out of it, and a redesigned CTA that couples to the loop the content opens. This is the most common mode for content-led growth.

### Mode 3: REVIEW

Score an existing micromagnet on the four-corner test. No rebuilding unless asked. Inputs:
- The magnet itself
- The handraiser post
- Optional: conversion-rate signal

Output: a verdict (where it falls on the matrix), a scorecard for the 5 build steps, and the single change that would unblock it.

## The Magnet Matrix

Two axes. The reader's nervous system runs this check before they raise their hand.

```
                Hot topic
                    |
                    |
   Trap/hassle -----+----- Safe/easy
                    |
                    |
                Not hot
```

- **Trap + hot:** "Free strategy session." Sales call in disguise. Dead.
- **Trap + cold:** "Subscribe for weekly tips." Effort with no payoff. Dead.
- **Safe + cold:** "Free 14-day course." Useful but the reader doesn't have time. Mostly dead.
- **Safe + hot:** the micromagnet. Specific result, no effort, no salesperson. The only quadrant that converts.

Every magnet you build must land safe + hot. If it drifts toward effort or sales, rework it.

## The 5-step build

This is the backbone. Skip a step and the magnet either doesn't get clicked or doesn't get used.

1. **Sell a splinter.** Pick one tiny painful symptom, not a root cause, not a strategy. The reader's index finger has a splinter; they want it out. They don't want a lecture on glove-wearing.
   - Test: can the reader feel the splinter in one sentence? If you need a paragraph, it's too big.

2. **Give the cheat code.** Don't teach. Give them the answers, like slipping someone the completed answer key before the test. Outcome over education. If they learn something, that's a side effect, not the goal.
   - Test: could the reader use this without reading instructions?

3. **Pick the delivery vehicle.** Match the splinter to the fastest possible tool. Menu in the next section.
   - Test: does the format itself signal "this is a tool, not homework"?

4. **Lead them home.** A funnel is a series of easy yeses. The micromagnet is yes #1. What's yes #2? Almost always the primary conversion from `brand.md`. If that's an email opt-in, the magnet inbox-delivers and the welcome sequence does the rest. If it's a signup, a booking, or an install, the magnet hands the reader straight into that first step. Sometimes yes #2 is "reply with a word" or "go watch this video."
   - Test: when the reader finishes the 2-minute use, what's the obvious next click?

5. **Generate demand with a handraiser post.** The post that gets the reader to ask for the magnet. Leo Burnett's 60-year-old formula: here's what I got, here's what it'll do for you, here's how to get it. Used in text posts, threads, carousels, video scripts. Doesn't matter the channel. Same formula.
   - Test: does the post name a splinter the reader felt this week?

## Delivery vehicles (the menu)

Listed in rough order of fit for AI-native, content-led projects. Reorder for your own project. The first three are the fastest to ship.

1. **Installable Claude skill.** The reader installs a skill into Claude Code or Claude.ai and runs it forever. High value for AI-native audiences. Example shapes: a lead-magnet-extractor skill, a landing-page-roaster skill, a story-finder skill. If you already have an internal skill that does the job, ship the public-facing little brother of it, the splinter-removing slice of the full tool.
2. **Cheatsheet PDF.** Single page, scannable, on-brand (follow the visual and house style in `brand.md`). Best for: teardowns, scorecards, decision trees, frameworks distilled to one page.
3. **Prompt pack.** A `.txt` or `.md` file of copy-paste prompts. Trivial to ship, unambiguous value, strong fit for AI-native audiences. Best for: sequences ("3 prompts to roast your landing page"), libraries ("12 starter prompts for your reader").
4. **Notion or Google Doc template.** Reader duplicates and uses. Best for: trackers, planners, swipe files, scripts.
5. **Custom GPT.** Higher build cost, but high credibility. Best for problems where the reader needs a few questions answered to get a tailored result. Reserve for splinters genuinely worth a GPT.

If none of those fit, the splinter is probably wrong. Reshape the splinter, not the vehicle.

## Title formulas (the Headline Workshop)

Six formulas that make a magnet sound like a tool instead of homework. Pick one, fill the brackets, run it past the AI-tells filter, ship.

1. **Steal my [strategy/tool] that [outcome] in [timeframe].**
   "Steal the 3-step closeout system a non-coder used to ship a paid tool in a weekend."
2. **The [X-minute] [thing] that solves [problem] instantly.**
   "The 5-minute newsletter edit that doubles reply rate."
3. **The [X-word] [thing] that [incredible result].**
   "The 7-word reply that turns a cold lead into a booked call."
4. **How I achieved [X] without [big pain point].**
   "How a first-time founder shipped a working app without writing a line of code."
5. **What [successful person] knows about [topic] that you don't.**
   "What total beginners know about shipping with AI that seasoned engineers keep missing."
6. **The simple [strategy/tool] that [achieves X] (and why it works).**
   "The simple thumbnail trick that lifts open rate 11% (and why it works)."

Default to formulas 1, 2, or 3. They sound most like tools.

Avoid: "ultimate guide," "complete blueprint," "everything you need to know." Those are dead-magnet titles.

## The handraiser post (Leo Burnett formula)

This is the post that gets the conversion. Same skeleton on every channel.

```
Here's what I got: [the thing, named with a title formula]
Here's what it'll do for you: [the splinter it removes, in the reader's language]
Here's how to get it: [the conversion mechanism, usually "do X and I'll send it"]
```

Three rules:
- The post speaks to the reader's situation and frustration, not the topic abstractly. "You're at 11pm with three half-finished prompts, none of them shipped" beats "creators struggle with consistency."
- The CTA reuses the title's language. If the magnet is "the 5-minute newsletter edit," the CTA says "send 5-minute" not "subscribe."
- No AI tells, no dashes, no "nobody [verb]" pseudo-contrarian. Run the same banned-phrase filter as every other public draft (`brand.md` house style, then `turma:anti-ai-linguo`).

## Amplify the handraiser (from emotion-craft)

The Leo Burnett skeleton is the floor. To make the post actually pull, layer the triggers from `turma:emotion-craft`, all of them true or none of them used:

- **Investment signaling.** Prove the effort. "The 43-page playbook I built over 18 months with 32 companies" beats "my free guide," because visible effort triggers reciprocity. Only if the effort is real.
- **Real social proof.** "I've sent this to 800+ founders this month" creates demand, but only if the number is real.
- **Genuine exclusivity.** "I normally only share this with clients" works if it is true, never as a manufactured line.
- **The emotional stack for the post:** curiosity gap (hook), then identity validation (name the reader's struggle and shift the blame off them), then tribal belonging (the sophisticated group vs the naive one, honestly), then investment signaling (the effort), then aspiration (an achievable outcome), then the low-friction ask ("comment WORD").

Hard line, and it is the same as this skill's existing rule: never invent proof, a number, scarcity, or urgency. Fake FOMO is the fastest way to burn trust. Real proof amplifies, fake proof detonates.

## Couple the magnet to the content (the EXTRACT play)

This is where most content leaves conversions on the table. You write a great piece, then staple a generic "subscribe" on the bottom.

In EXTRACT mode, find the magnet hiding inside the content first.

- A case-study piece (a customer who got a result, a founder's turnaround)? The magnet is the checklist or template the protagonist actually used. Extract it as a skill or PDF.
- A teardown (someone else's launch, a competitor's funnel)? The magnet is the scorecard you used to evaluate it.
- A framework piece (a named method, a step-by-step system)? The magnet is the prompt pack or installable skill that runs the framework.
- A list piece ("7 prompts I run every Monday")? The magnet IS the list, packaged as a downloadable.

If you can't find the magnet inside the content, the content might be too abstract. Reshape the content, not just the CTA.

## Quality gate before delivery

Before any micromagnet ships in a draft to outbox:

1. **Matrix check.** Does it land safe + hot? If it drifts toward "free 30-min call" or "subscribe for tips," scrap and rebuild.
2. **Splinter check.** Can the reader feel the splinter in one sentence?
3. **2-minute check.** Can the reader use the magnet in under 2 minutes after they get it?
4. **Coupling check.** Does the handraiser post's language match the magnet's title? Does the magnet match the splinter the post named?
5. **AI tells filter.** No "furthermore," "moreover," "delve into," "leverage," "elevate." No dashes (em, en, or any unicode dash). No "nobody [verb]" pseudo-contrarian. Apply `brand.md` house style, then run `turma:anti-ai-linguo` as the final pass.
6. **Scoreboard check.** Which number on the `brand.md` scoreboard does this move? If none, ask why.

## Procedure

When invoked, work through these steps in order. Show the work in the output so the owner can audit.

1. **State the mode.** CREATE / EXTRACT / REVIEW. State the target outcome (the primary conversion from `brand.md`).

2. **Resolve source material.**
   - CREATE: confirm splinter + reader. If thin, ask the owner one question before drafting.
   - EXTRACT: read the draft once for the loop, once for the splinter hiding inside.
   - REVIEW: read the magnet + handraiser post twice. Don't rebuild yet.

3. **Run the 5 steps.** Even in REVIEW mode, write the map. The map shows what's missing more than the prose does.

4. **Draft or score.**
   - CREATE / EXTRACT: produce the title, delivery vehicle (with build spec if it's a skill/GPT), the artifact or scaffold, and the handraiser post. Then run the quality gate.
   - REVIEW: score each step on a 1-3 scale (1 = missing/broken, 2 = present but weak, 3 = strong). Identify the single weakest step. Suggest one specific change.

5. **Hand off.**
   - CREATE / EXTRACT: artifact goes to `outbox/` with status `review-needed`. Note which step did the heavy lifting and what the one risk is.
   - REVIEW: scorecard back to invoker. If the owner asks for the fix, switch to CREATE/EXTRACT for the weak step only.

## Examples to study, not to copy

- **Tony Allen's source magnet:** a custom GPT that suggests a magnet and writes the social post for it. The reported result on his own launch was 1,256 emails in 48 hours at $0.27 per lead. Textbook safe + hot.
- **A case-study SOP:** if a piece profiles someone who shipped a result, packaging the actual SOP or checklist they used as a Notion template is a micromagnet. The piece itself is the handraiser post.
- **Your internal tools:** any internal skill or workflow is a candidate for a "little brother" public version. A story framework becomes a public "story scorer" skill. A naming process becomes a public "name-the-thing" skill. The internal version is the full tool. The public micromagnet is the splinter-removing slice.

## Calibration: what good looks like

A micromagnet that earns its keep:
- Lands in the safe + hot quadrant.
- Reader knows what they're getting in under 5 seconds of reading the post.
- Reader uses it in under 2 minutes of receiving it.
- Title sounds like a tool, not a course.
- Handraiser post names the reader's exact 11pm scene.
- The primary conversion from `brand.md` is the natural next step, not a bolt-on.
- At minimum a 3% post-to-conversion rate. Above 8% is a hit. Reuse hits until they stop converting, then retire.

## Hard rules

- Reads the project's `brand.md` before drafting. Splinter, voice, and conversion goal come from there, never hardcoded.
- Never edits operating canon (`SOUL.md`, `IDENTITY.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, `SPEC.md`, `memory/`).
- Never published autonomously. Every magnet and every handraiser post goes to `outbox/` for the owner's review before shipping (Yellow tier).
- Never invents proof. If the magnet promises a result, the result must be real (a real metric, a real customer's outcome, a documented benchmark). No invented case studies.
- Never breaks the project's visual and house style when shipping a cheatsheet. Follow `brand.md`, no exceptions.
- Never recommends a "free strategy call" or "book a 15-min chat" CTA. That's the trap quadrant.
- No AI tells, no dashes. Run `turma:anti-ai-linguo` as the final pass.
