# thumbnail-craft

YouTube is a click-and-watch platform. The thumbnail has one job: win the click without lying about what is inside. It is a visual hook. The psychology of the click is the same as any hook (`turma:emotion-craft`); this skill owns the part that is specific to a 16:9 image, the composition, lighting, and simplicity that make the hook land.

**Before you start:** read the project's `brand.md` for the channel's locked thumbnail style, the creator's face and format, the brand colors, and the primary conversion. A thumbnail should be instantly recognizable as this channel's. If a `youtube-connector` is wired, pull the channel's and the niche's outliers first (see Outlier research).

Source: distilled from David Altizer's thumbnail-design interview (clients include Curtis Connor, Jolly, Max Fosh; ~350M views on his thumbnails), then cross-checked and enriched with Paddy Galloway's packaging framework, Derral Eves' The YouTube Formula, and the current 2026 high-CTR thumbnail consensus. The craft is theirs. The examples are illustrative, not to copy. Blog-sourced CTR percentages are directional, not citable law.

## The packaging principle (design the click before you shoot)

The title and thumbnail are one package, and the package comes first, before the video is shot. Packaging (the title, the thumbnail, and the description) sells the click; the first thirty seconds sell the watch (Paddy Galloway). Win the click first, or nothing downstream matters. Work backwards: start from a compelling package (often reverse-engineered from an outlier), then make the video deliver it. Mr. Beast saw an outlier thumbnail of a train going into a pit, made the thumbnail, then dug a hole and dropped a train in it.

**Confirm the click.** The video must deliver what the thumbnail promises. If the most compelling moment is revealed late, add a short teaser at the very start so the viewer sees the promise is real. Never leave them feeling tricked.

**Clickbait is okay, misleading is not.** You can overexaggerate the thumbnail past what is literally in the video, as long as the viewer ends up satisfied that the promise was true. The instant they feel deceived, you lose them. This is the same guardrail as `turma:emotion-craft`: earn the emotion, never fake it.

## The click psychology (compose emotion-craft)

The best thumbnail idea is a mystery nobody knew they needed to know: take something the viewer assumes they understand and subvert it, so they feel uncomfortable not knowing the answer. "What if mini forks were made of salt?" "Is it green or is it yellow?" (a Gatorade bottle). The pattern: "here is a thing you know, but little did you know THIS is the truth."

The mid-action moment is the strongest visual version. Show the thing in the middle of happening, not before, not after (the Far Side paper-bag-behind-the-bomb-scientist frame, Mark Rober's elephant-toothpaste explosion mid-eruption). The viewer has to click to resolve it.

For the full trigger palette (identity validation, tribal belonging, aspiration, and the rest), run `turma:emotion-craft`. The thumbnail usually pulls the curiosity gap plus one more.

## The visual craft (what is specific to thumbnails)

### 1. Composition
- **Rule of thirds.** Overlay a tic-tac-toe grid. Put the points of interest on the four crossing points, not dead center. Altizer keeps the grid on as an overlay while editing. Off-center reads as balanced and intentional.
- **Visual weight.** Balance the frame like an old scale. A face pushed to one side needs something (an object, text, or deliberate negative space) to balance the other side. A stray plant or bright object throws it off; move or remove it.
- **Centered is a style, not a default.** Dead-center works only when the background elements balance it (the Wes Anderson / center-frame cinematic look).
- Study movie posters and cinematography (both framed 16:9) for composition, not portrait photography.
- **Contrast.** A bright or high-contrast subject against a darker, simpler background makes it pop in a crowded feed. Complementary colors on the key element pull the eye. A human figure plus one clear object is a consistently high-impact pairing.

### 2. Lighting
- **Flat and bright.** Light the face clearly (a key above the camera plus soft fill, or sit next to a window). You can add shadow in post easily; you cannot remove harsh shadow or darkness. Humans react to humans, so the face has to read.
- **Intentional thumbnail photos, not screenshots.** A dark selfie or a soft video screenshot is the amateur tell. Shoot the thumbnail on purpose.
- **Capture in RAW** (Apple ProRAW or Android raw) for the cleanest, least-processed shot. It removes the phone's HDR, noise reduction, and face-smoothing that make thumbnails look plasticky.
- **The face does the work, naturally.** The brain has a dedicated face-processing region, so a clear, well-lit face pulls the eye hard. Match the expression to the video's emotion, but keep it natural. Exaggerated faces worked in 2015; genuine expression wins now.

### 3. Simplicity (lower the cognitive load)
- **~3 elements max.** Subject, one key object or background, one short piece of text. A pile of many objects (the "I bought every X" look) counts as one unified element.
- **Remove distractions.** Logos on hats, lav mics, background clutter (poles, bright cars, the mouse and cup on the desk). Use generative fill to erase them. Keep the podcast mic for a podcast, though, it signals the format.
- **Clarity over density.** Every element the eye has to process is a tax on the click.

### 4. Text
- **Fewest words possible.** Two lines, about four words. The title carries the rest.
- **Third-grade reading level.** Simple words (bad, good, viral, anything). Big words and long strings raise the cognitive load and cost the click.

## The style buckets (pick one and lock it in brand.md)

A channel wants a repeatable, recognizable style. Pick the bucket that fits the niche and the brand, then keep it consistent so the audience recognizes it instantly.

- **Realism.** Clean, photo-real, intentional (Altizer's main style, current Mr. Beast).
- **Versus (A vs B).** Two sides opposed across the frame.
- **Center-frame cinematic.** Subject dead center, background balanced.
- **Podcast.** Guest or host mid-sentence, a short compelling quote beside them, a branded repeatable background (keep the mic).
- **Screenshot / raw.** Minimal editing. Works for finance and commentary niches where the content is the draw and highly edited thumbnails would look out of place.
- **Reaction.** The person reacting big, with what they are reacting to behind them (exaggerated).
- **Gear / product.** Just a beautiful shot of the product, often no person.
- **Scrapbook.** Many elements collaged (Curtis Connor), treated as one busy unified object.

Trend note: the market moved from over-processed plastic to raw-authentic and now to realism (photo-real but intentional, pores and sharpness intact, cleanly composed). Read the current niche before committing.

## The asset workflow (when working with a creator)

- **Ask for well-lit RAW shots** against a clean, simple background (a white wall cuts out easily), with a few poses and the right expression. Not a dark screenshot.
- **You direct the composition**, not the creator. Cutting out and repositioning with generative fill and subject-selection is the designer's job.
- **Pull reference images** from outliers in the niche for the concept.

## AI as a faster Photoshop

Image tools do the slow pixel work: generative fill for cleanup, face-consistent generators (Nano Banana and similar) for placing the creator into a scene, Midjourney for backgrounds and concepts. Verify any tool's current parameters before relying on them, do not invent flags. The human levers stay human: the idea, the click psychology, and the composition. thumbnail-craft's output is the concept plus a composition and text spec plus either a shotlist for the creator or an image-generation prompt. It does not push the final pixels itself; that is a tool step.

## Test the package (do not guess)

Do not ship one thumbnail on instinct. Draft several title-and-thumbnail pairs (strong creators draft anywhere from a handful to a dozen or more), then test three genuinely different concepts, not three tints of one design, with YouTube's native thumbnail A/B test. Each variant should test a different idea (a different mystery, a different composition) so the result teaches you something. Keep the winner, and feed the pattern back through the learnings loop. A concept that keeps winning across videos is a technique worth logging.

## Outlier research (compose power-law + youtube-connector + viral-teardown)

An outlier is a video that beat its own channel's average, not one with high absolute views. A small channel's 1M-view video against its usual 3K is a huge outlier: the concept works. Find outliers in the niche (via `turma:youtube-connector`, or tools like ViewStats, VidIQ, One of 10), tear the winning thumbnail down with `turma:viral-teardown` to extract the pattern, then recreate the pattern (never the content) for the brand. This is `turma:power-law`'s double-down applied to packaging.

## Modes

State the mode at the top of the output.

### Mode 1: CREATE
Design a thumbnail (and its title). Inputs: the video idea, the channel (brand.md), any assets or the shooting plan. Output: the concept (the mystery or hook), the chosen style bucket, the composition (what sits on which third, the visual-weight plan), the text (2 lines, simple), the lighting and asset requirements or an AI prompt, and the "confirm the click" note.

### Mode 2: EVALUATE
Score an existing thumbnail. Inputs: the thumbnail (and title), optionally the CTR signal. Output: a scorecard across composition, lighting, simplicity, text, click psychology, and clickbait-vs-misleading, plus the single change that would lift the click.

## Hard rules

- Reads `brand.md`. The style, the face, the colors, and the format come from there, and the thumbnail must look like this channel.
- Clickbait is fine, misleading is not. The video must deliver the thumbnail's promise.
- Simplicity wins. Cut every element that does not earn its place.
- Never fabricate a result or a metric put on a thumbnail. Real numbers only.
- Do not invent AI-tool parameters. Verify or disclaim.
- Composes, does not re-derive: `turma:emotion-craft` for the psychology, `turma:viral-teardown` for outlier teardowns, `turma:power-law` for the outlier double-down.
