# power-law

Most people grow content like they grow a savings account. Post twice as much, get twice the followers. Work twice as hard, get twice the result. Linear. It is the wrong model for anything that matters.

Attention, music, startups, and wealth all run on power laws: a tiny fraction of events captures almost all of the value. 1% of artists take ~90% of Spotify streams. A handful of YC companies dwarf the other five thousand combined. The top 1% of US households hold more wealth than the bottom 50%. Content is the same. A few pieces carry almost all the reach. The rest is rounding error.

This is not a reason to despair. It is an instruction set. You stop optimizing the average and start playing the distribution on purpose.

## The TBP reframe (lead with this, it is ours)

The power law is usually told as bad news for the little guy. Most efforts die, you cannot predict the winner, the top 1% eats everything. The establishment uses it to say do not bother.

Flip it. The power law only beat regular people because each lottery ticket used to be expensive. You could not write 500 good posts, cut 50 videos, ship 9 products. AI drops the cost per ticket toward zero. For the first time a regular person can buy enough shots for the power law to work in their favor.

The power law did not change. The price of a ticket did. That is the whole TBP thesis in one sentence, and the deli owner who shipped in three days is the proof: he did not beat the odds by being special, he took a shot that used to cost a dev team, for free.

- A piece just over-performed and you need a compounding plan before the signal goes cold.

- Reading the dashboards. The skill reframes what "most posts did nothing" means.

## When NOT to invoke

## The law (use the verified numbers, flag the soft ones)

Repeatable, sourced, safe to cite:
- Top 1% of Spotify artists take ~90% of streams. Top 0.1% take over half.
- Spotify pays roughly $0.003 to $0.005 per stream (avg ~$0.0038), on a streamshare model, so there is no fixed per-stream rate.
- Y Combinator: ~5,600 companies, ~$600B combined value, and the top four (Airbnb, DoorDash, Coinbase, Instacart) are ~84% of the public market cap.
- Peter Thiel: $500K into Facebook in 2004 became over $1.1B (~2,200x).
- The "Babe Ruth Effect" is real VC doctrine, not guru talk. VCs lose on 80 to 90% of bets and still win, because one winner pays for all the losses.
- US wealth: top 1% hold ~31%, bottom 50% hold ~2.5%.

Soft, do NOT state as fact:
- "90,000 songs uploaded a day" (Spotify's own figure is ~60,000; the ~100K+ number is all platforms combined).
- "Median song gets 30 plays" (directionally true: ~45.6M tracks never played, 87% under 1,000 streams, but the exact figure is unsourced).
- "1% of posts get 90% of engagement" (an extrapolation from one creator's account, not a measured law).

## The barbell, mapped to TBP

Do not spread effort evenly. Split it to the extremes.

**Concentrated craft bets** (a few, all-in):
- The Saturday newsletter.
- Pillar pages and hero lead magnets.
- The one piece a week we genuinely research, illustrate, and double-edit.

**Volume lottery tickets** (many, cheap, posted fast):
- YouTube Shorts (the cta-machine).
- IG and TikTok gallery carousels (ghostshelf).
- Daily X and LinkedIn posts.

The mistake is judging a ticket by a craft-bet standard, or pouring craft-bet effort into a ticket. A Short that took four minutes is a ticket. Treat it like one. Nine of ten doing nothing is the distribution working, not the engine failing.

## The four moves

1. **Produce at volume on the cheap surfaces.** Each post is a ticket. If P(hit) is ~0.5% per post, ten posts give you a ~5% chance of at least one hit, a hundred give ~39%, five hundred give ~92%. You cannot pick the winner in advance, so you buy enough tickets to make a winner statistically inevitable.
2. **Invest disproportionately in the bets.** 80% of output is good-enough, posted fast, minimal editing. 20% gets all the creative energy: research, a real reframe, original visuals, the math shown. Those are the swings.
3. **When one hits, double down before the signal cools.** A viral piece is the most valuable market signal you will ever get. It tells you exactly what readers want right now. Do not move on to the next random thing. Milk it: follow-up, thread, a series, a micromagnet or skill built around it, a newsletter edition, eventually a product. PolymarketEye started as one viral hit that the author refused to let go cold.
4. **Survive the 99%.** Most efforts produce near-zero direct results. Runway is what lets the outlier arrive. For TBP that means keeping the engines fueled through the flat stretch: do not let the Shorts queue or the gallery queue run dry (the cta-machine queue cliff is exactly this risk).

## Content architecture (how to build a craft bet with outlier potential)

The essay is itself its own best evidence: 1.8M views from a piece built on a repeatable structure. Steal the structure for the 20% bets. Six parts:

1. **Confession hook.** Open with a vulnerable, specific, slightly embarrassing admission plus a shocking number gap. "A meme I made in four minutes beat a two-week article 17M views to 200." Bissuh has the real scar tissue to do this honestly.
2. **The wrong model, named.** "Here is how you think the world works." State the linear belief the reader holds, then break it. This is the story-craft reframe in the wild.
3. **Stack three or four domains.** Show the same principle in content, then music, then startups, then wealth. By the third domain the reader stops arguing and accepts it as a law.
4. **Show the math inline.** Concrete expected-value beats adjectives. "Option A: 80% x $50M = $40M. Option B: 5% x $10B = $500M." Make the calculation visible as a payoff.
5. **Include the depressing corollary.** Admit the downside honestly (most of your effort produces nothing). Counterintuitively, owning the dark side is what makes the upside believable instead of hype.
6. **Explicit action turn.** End with a clearly labeled "what this means for you" section and a short, numbered set of moves.

## Three modes

State the mode at the top of your output.

### Mode 1: DIAGNOSE
Given a piece, a surface, or a performance result, classify and judge it. Inputs: the artifact or the metric, and what we expected. Output: craft-bet or volume-ticket, the standard it should be held to, and the call (kill, ship-cheap, or invest), plus what the result actually means under a power law.

### Mode 2: ARCHITECT
Build a craft bet designed for outlier potential. Inputs: the topic or angle and the target outcome. Output: the six-part architecture filled in as a skeleton, with the confession hook drafted and the domains to stack chosen. Hand off to story-craft for line-level work and micromagnet-craft for the CTA.

### Mode 3: DOUBLE-DOWN
A piece just popped. Inputs: the hit, the signal (what specifically resonated), and the surface. Output: a compounding plan to execute within 48 hours: the follow-up, the series, the micromagnet or skill, the newsletter edition, and the product seed, ranked by speed-to-ship.
